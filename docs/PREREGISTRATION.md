# Pre-registration: synthetic crisis-severity and trainee-conduct study

**Status:** frozen at the commit that adds this file. Any later change to the
rules below is recorded in the "Amendments" section with a date and reason,
never edited in place. Test-split results were not computed before this commit
(the `runs/` directory is gitignored; the report records the commit of every
run file's first line).

## Purpose

Measure what TypeSafe's Jev model (`jev-1.13.0`) adds on top of two deterministic
floors, against LLM judges given the identical questions, on synthetic data
large enough for confidence intervals to mean something.

## Hypotheses

- **H1 (crisis).** On the test split, lexicon ∪ Jev at the dev-chosen threshold has an
  under-triage rate no higher than lexicon ∪ Sonnet 4.5 (the incumbent), with a
  false-fire rate on `none` items no higher than the incumbent's.
- **H2 (trainee).** On the test split, regex ∪ Jev at the dev-chosen threshold has a
  higher per-harm F1 than regex alone, with no more false flags on clean and
  near-miss negatives than 5%.
- **H3 (omission).** Jev's "asked directly" probability separates `never` arcs from
  `immediately` and `after_validating` arcs with accuracy above 0.9 at the
  dev-chosen grace and threshold.
- **H4 (calibration).** Jev's native probabilities have lower ECE than every LLM's
  verbalised probabilities on the same items. (Comparable only across
  elicitation methods as a descriptive claim; see "Elicitation".)

## Data

Generated by `jevlab gen` from the grid in `src/jevlab/synth/spec.py`, by two
families: Anthropic (`claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929`) and OpenAI (`gpt-5.5`). The
intended label of every item is fixed by its grid cell before generation. Items
are not edited by hand. Exclusions applied before splitting: near-duplicates
(token-set Jaccard ≥ 0.8; the later item is marked `dup_of`) and fixture leaks
(any 8-gram shared with `fixtures/`). Excluded items stay in the file, flagged,
and are omitted from every metric.

Split: 20% dev / 80% test, stratified by (use case, cell, family), arcs kept
whole, seed 0. Thresholds and grace are chosen on dev only and frozen in
`docs/THRESHOLDS.md` before any test-split scoring.

Cross-family verification: each item's verifier label is the hard label given by
the other family's generator model acting as a judge (Sonnet 4.5 for OpenAI-
generated items, gpt-5.5 for Anthropic-generated). `contested` = verifier ≠
intended. Contested items are kept; every headline table has a sensitivity row
that excludes them.

Human audit: the owner labels 200 test items blind (text only) with
`jevlab audit`; agreement human-vs-intended and human-vs-model is reported. The
owner is not a clinician and the report says so.

## Judges

| Provider | Models | Sampling | Elicitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| TypeSafe | jev-1.13.0 | n/a | native |
| Anthropic | claude-haiku-4-5, claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929, claude-sonnet-5, claude-opus-5 | temperature 0 on 4.5 models; provider default with adaptive thinking at effort low on Sonnet 5 and Opus 5 | verbalised |
| OpenAI | gpt-4.1, gpt-5.4-mini, gpt-5.5 | temperature 0 on gpt-4.1; reasoning_effort low, provider default sampling on gpt-5* | verbalised |
| Local | qwen36-mtplx, qwen36-35b-mtplx, gemma4-31b-mtplx | temperature 0, thinking off | verbalised |

Every LLM receives the prompt rendered by `jevlab.judges.prompt.build_messages`
from the same question objects Jev receives. No examples, no calibration hints.
Parse failures are recorded and counted; a single re-ask is allowed and flagged.

## Elicitation

`native` (Jev), `verbalised` (the probabilities the LLM writes in its JSON),
`ksample5` (frequency over five samples at temperature 1, on a 300-item subset,
models that accept temperature only), `logprob` (exploratory; gpt-4.1 and the
mlx-served Qwen build). Brier and ECE are compared only within one method;
accuracy, F1, kappa, under-triage and false-fire rates are comparable across all.

## Hard label and policy rows

Hard label for every judge = argmax of its distribution (Jev: the Score/Choice
probabilities; Noul ≥ 0.5). Policy rows apply the phase-1 rules:
`union(floor, tail_level(p, τ))` for crisis and `floor or p ≥ τ` for trainee,
with τ chosen on dev by: the lowest τ in {0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5} (crisis) or
{0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9} (trainee) whose false-fire rate on dev negatives does not
exceed the floor's, breaking ties toward lower under-triage / higher F1.

## Primary metrics

- Crisis: under-triage rate (final level below intended) and exact-level
  accuracy, on single items and on arcs (highest level reached).
- Trainee: per-harm F1 and false-flag rate on negatives.
- Omission: fire/silent accuracy over arcs.
- All with 2,000-resample bootstrap 95% CIs, clustered by arc where items share
  one; paired differences vs Jev resample the same clusters. A difference is
  reported as a result only when its CI excludes 0.

## Secondary

Brier and ECE with 10-bin reliability diagrams per elicitation; cost per 1,000
calls from `prices.yaml`; latency p50/p95 from the 200-item synchronous subset
per hosted model; parse-failure and re-ask counts; results stratified by
generator family and by contested flag.

## Amendments

- **A1 (2026-09-17, before any test-split scoring).** Gemma 4 31B is measured at
  roughly 28 s per call on this machine, which would put the synthetic sets
  alone at about 60 hours. It runs on the clinical fixtures and the AnnoMI local
  tier only; the two Qwen builds run everything. The local-model rows for the
  synthetic sets are therefore Qwen only.
- **A2 (2026-09-17, dev split, before any test-split scoring).** The first dev
  run showed that both generators had written the trainee "clean" cells as
  replies that commit the conduct (Jev flagged 118/149 Anthropic and 139/149
  OpenAI clean items; near-miss and positive cells were as intended). The 60
  clean cells were regenerated with a prompt that states the reply must not
  commit the conduct; item ids now carry a hash of the generated text so no
  regenerated item can inherit a cached judgment. The trainee threshold chosen
  on the defective dev split was discarded and is re-chosen on the corrected
  one. No test-split result had been computed.
- **A3 (2026-09-17, dev split, before any test-split scoring).** Crisis arcs
  and omission arcs have one arc per grid cell, so the stratum (use case, cell,
  family) held a single unit and the split rule sent every arc to test. Arc
  strata now pool over register and distractor (crisis: family × level ×
  language; omission: family × asks), so about 20% of arcs are in dev and the
  thresholds are chosen on arcs as well as single messages. Item ids and texts
  are unchanged; the Jev thresholds were re-chosen on the corrected dev split.
- **A4 (2026-09-17, during test-split scoring).** The OpenAI account reached its
  monthly credit limit part-way through the test split. Complete on OpenAI: all
  three models on AnnoMI, the clinical fixtures and the dev split; gpt-5.4-mini on
  the full test split; gpt-5.5 on the crisis and trainee test sets. Not scored
  unless credit is added: the remainder of gpt-5.5's omission set, gpt-4.1's test
  split, and gpt-4.1's k-sample subset. Rows already scored are unaffected; the
  reports list missing judges under "not scored (coverage)".
- **A5 (2026-09-17, during test-split scoring).** The five Opus 5 batches (test
  split and both AnnoMI sets) were cancelled after four hours at 0% progress, to
  cap spend. Opus 5 keeps its clinical-fixture and dev-split results; it is
  "not scored" on the synthetic test split and AnnoMI.
- **A6 (2026-09-17, during test-split scoring).** On the one-question AnnoMI
  client task, Qwen3.6-35B returned the answer body without the question id
  (`{"choice": ..., "p": {...}}`), so 247 otherwise-valid answers were recorded
  as parse failures; no other judge was affected (the hosted ones use structured
  output). The parser now accepts an unwrapped answer when a task has exactly
  one question, which is unambiguous, and flags every such row
  `unwrapped_answer`; multi-question tasks are unchanged and still require the
  ids. The re-ask prompt now names the required top-level keys. Affected rows
  were re-judged rather than re-parsed in place. The leniency applies to every
  judge alike and its use is counted in the reports.
- **A7 (2026-09-17, local tier scope).** The local tier is Qwen3.6-27B, which is
  complete on every experiment (445/445 AnnoMI client, 555/555 therapist, 305 and
  101 clinical fixtures, 1,147 + 894 + 366 synthetic). Qwen3.6-35B is complete on
  the clinical fixtures and AnnoMI therapist and appears there; its remaining sets
  were abandoned after its server degraded twice, so it is not scored on them.
  Gemma 4 31B produced no usable rows (the meter cannot relay it, and the
  direct-port fix arrived after its slot) and is not scored anywhere. The
  question the local tier answers is whether a workstation model can do this
